So you think you’re neurospicy

A brain dump of resources for friends who think they might be neurodivergent.

Henry Franks June 5, 2026 ~15 min read

This is a brain dump of resources I’ve put together for friends that think they might be neurodivergent (ND). I’m AuDHD (autistic + adhd, which combine in a distinct and separate type to the individual flavours), so it’s heavily biased that way. I can only speak on the things I’ve learned and experienced.

Ground rules:

  • Everyone’s manifestation of ND is unique. There are over a thousand genes implicated in the development of ADHD alone. If you’ve met one neurospicy person, you’ve met one neurospicy person; they will manifest a complex spectrum of things in a completely unique way and experience the world in a way that can’t be generalised to “all” ND people
  • The self-learning journey is worth it, but it comes with challenges as well as benefit. Prepare for difficult self-reflection. For me, it has been wholly and transcendentally life changing. I feel at peace and happy with myself in a way that never felt attainable before this journey. But it has been hard work, and with some painful times.
  • What has worked for me is to read wide and deep, to learn as much as possible. If something resonates with you (you’ll know it when you feel it), pull that thread. If it doesn’t resonate with you, it’s probably not as relevant to your form of neurospiciness.
  • There’s a lot of disinformation out there, especially on social media. Don’t throw the baby out with the bathwater though, because there are also really valuable nuggets out there too, especially on practical day to day tips and tricks for coping with deficits
  • There is huge ND community debate about “superpower”-first identity and “disability”-first identity. You can choose what identity, if any, resonates with you. I find aspects of my neurospiciness a superpower, a set of traits that I’m proud of, that have brought me success and/or joy; and aspects of it distinctly disabling. Accepting the disability part of my neurological profile was challenging for me, but with that acceptance has come much less shame, much better ability to advocate for and stand firm on my needs, tweaks to my professional and personal life that has helped a lifetime cycle of burnout, and more.
  • The evidence shows that the biggest and most reliable quality of life improvements come from medication (for ADHDers) plus coaching and therapy. Medication helps you unlock the pilots seat of your brain a bit more, but you still need to learn how to fly the plane. The toolbox that experts have helped me build was entirely unintuitive and non-obvious to me, going against all the neurotypical habits, routines, approaches and so on that an upbringing in a society built for brains that work differently teaches you. Unlearning those and learning new tools was hard — often frustrating, because often the tools are SO SIMPLE once you learn them.
  • Speaking of tools, again, you will have to experiment. I’ve tried probably hundreds of different tricks and techniques; I’d say my toolbox is a few tens of techniques strong. But those work so well for piloting my specific unique brain that it has been fairly easy to internalise them over time and make them routine.

Link dump

This is several years of bookmarks from my raindrop collection, organised by Claude.

1. Understanding the ADHD brain & lived experience

  • How Adults with ADHD Think: ADHD and the Nervous System — William Dodson’s foundational portrait of the ADHD nervous system as interest-driven rather than “attention-deficient,” reframing symptoms as features of a differently-wired mind.
  • Thoughts on ADHD — LessWrong — A rationalist’s introspective model linking rejection sensitivity, novelty-seeking, and time dysregulation into a coherent personal theory of attention.
  • ADHD wiki — Explaining ADHD with memes — A self-discovery narrative showing how memes and hyperfocus, not clinical checklists, often surface adult ADHD recognition.
  • Productivity Tools are Taxed — Adam Grant — The counter-intuitive argument that stillness and idleness, not more tools, are the most powerful levers for an ADHD brain stuck in a “sped-up” state.
  • Celebrating Atypical Minds — A community-voiced affirmation that atypical cognition is worth celebrating rather than correcting.

Worth knowing: ADDitude (additudemag.com) is essentially the ADHD community bible — the nervous-system piece above is one of many. Reading widely across the whole site is heartily recommended, not just this single article.

2. Recognition, diagnosis & medication

  • Recognizing ADHD in Adults — Heather Brannon (TEDxHeritageGreen) — A primary-care doctor’s talk on how adult ADHD actually presents; the clip that was a real turning point in recognising these traits in yourself.
  • The ADHD Centre (UK) — The private clinic I used for a formal diagnosis and route to medication: efficient but impersonal and fairly checklist-driven, so go in already well-informed about the criteria and process and treat it as a diagnosis/meds route rather than a source of support — best used once you’re confident with everything else here.

3. Executive function, stress & burnout

  • Executive Function Disorder & ADHD | ADDA — Clarifies the overlap and distinction between ADHD and executive dysfunction across memory, attention, and self-regulation.
  • Adults with ADHD Are More Vulnerable to Daily Stressors — Coach Linda Walker — Cites cortisol/heart-rate research showing ADHDers physiologically experience ordinary stressors more intensely.
  • Feeling Burnt Out? I’m Not Surprised — Coach Linda Walker — Argues ADHDers are 3–6x more prone to burnout, and that recovery must treat the underlying ADHD, not just the symptoms.
  • Toward Neurodivergent-Aware Productivity (arXiv) — A research framework for AI/systems design that accompanies rather than corrects ADHD professionals, centred on body-doubling and emotional safety.
  • Time blindness (key concept, personal note) — The ADHD difficulty with sensing the passage of time; worth naming explicitly because it underlies so much of the lateness, task-paralysis and “now / not-now” thinking that the resources above describe.

4. Sleep, flow & regulation — the non-negotiable basics

  • The role of the circadian system in ADHD (Bijlenga et al., 2019) — A clinical paper on how the circadian/sleep system is bound up with ADHD — the evidence behind the conviction that sleep isn’t a side issue, it’s everything.
  • Flow state as meditation (personal practice) — Standard “sit still and clear your mind” meditation rarely works for ADHD brains; getting into a flow-state activity that is not tied to productivity (for you, DJing) is meditation that works for us. Make protected time for it.

5. ADHD productivity strategies & tools

  • How to find balance with an ADHD brain — ADDept — The “colour-coded energy” model (and the “Red-Blue slide”) for protecting the tasks that actually refuel executive function.
  • 5 Easy Steps to Prioritize with ADHD — ADDept — Practical prioritisation that accepts “it will not all get done” and minimises the executive-function cost of deciding.
  • Claude productivity coach (Logan’s project instructions) — A worked ADHD-coaching prompt set explaining why standard GTD fails ADHD brains and how to adapt it (dopamine, hyperfocus, body doubling).
  • L-Theanine with Coffee — Adam Grant — A focus/anxiety hack pairing L-theanine with caffeine to smooth out the jitters while improving attention.
  • Validate, then respond — Adam Grant — A communication strategy (filed among ADHD tactics) for leading with validation before problem-solving.
  • Answering messages by first and last day — Adam Grant — A batching heuristic to tame the executive load of an overflowing inbox.
  • Semantic Trees — Adam Grant — A knowledge-structuring tool for offloading and organising thought.
  • Disorganized colleagues, own your chaotic brilliance! — Reframes messiness as a creativity and risk-taking asset, pushing back on the bias toward tidiness and linearity.

6. Masking, identity & the neurodiversity movement

  • ADHD Masking: Does Hiding Your Symptoms Help or Harm? — ADDA — Explains the costs of camouflaging ADHD traits and where masking helps versus harms.
  • Autism Is an Identity, Not a Disease — An inside view of the neurodiversity movement’s reframing of autism from pathology to identity.
  • Personal Brands, Identity and Perception Management — A lens on identity-as-performance that maps onto the mechanics of masking and self-presentation.
  • The Neurodivergent Community For Badasses (I Am Paying Attention) — A community and training hub offering an unapologetic, strengths-first take on living with ADHD and autism.

7. Autism — theory & cognition

  • Me and Monotropism: A unified theory of autism | BPS — Introduces monotropism (attention pooled into few interests) as a single explanatory thread for autistic experience.
  • How an understudied trait has skewed autism studies for decades | Spectrum — Shows how overlooking a key trait has biased decades of autism research.
  • Autism Checklist of DOOM — A lived-experience inventory of autistic traits that goes well beyond the clinical diagnostic shorthand.
  • Results and Analysis of the Autistic Not Weird 2022 Autism Survey — Large-scale community survey data on how autistic people and families actually feel about diagnosis, language, and acceptance.
  • “Autistic people can’t acknowledge when they are wrong” — A first-person rebuttal to a common stereotype, unpacking the communication mismatch behind it.
  • Autistic Purdue professor accused of being AI for lacking ‘warmth’ — A real-world illustration of how neurotypical “warmth” norms penalise direct autistic communication.

8. Autistic burnout, sensory experience & PDA

  • An Autistic Burnout — Thinking Person’s Guide to Autism — A firsthand account distinguishing autistic burnout from ordinary exhaustion or depression.
  • Autism and Anxiety vs Sensory Overload — Helps tease apart sensory overload from anxiety, which often look alike but need different responses.
  • PDA Relationships — How a Pathological/Persistent Demand Avoidance profile shapes relationships and how to navigate it.
  • Declarative Language Handbook — Communication guidance (especially around PDA) on using declarative rather than demand-based language — with nuance on where indirectness backfires.

9. Neurodivergence at work — accommodations & inclusion

  • On Focus, Attention Pathogens and Autism in the Tech Workplace — An engineer’s account of protecting focus and managing autistic attention in tech environments.
  • Creating an Autistic-Friendly Workplace · Public Neurodiversity Support Center — A practical, autistic-authored playbook for redesigning workplace norms.
  • Autistic adults’ views and experiences of requesting workplace adjustments in the UK (PMC) — Qualitative research on the barriers autistic employees face when asking for accommodations.
  • The Positive Effect of Workplace Accommodation on Creative Performance (Europe PMC) — Evidence that accommodations boost creative output for employees with and without disabilities.
  • From ADHD To ASD, How These Women Navigate Neurodiversity At Work (Refinery29) — Women’s first-person accounts of managing neurodivergence in professional settings.
  • How GitHub supports neurodiverse employees — A concrete corporate case study (the “Neurocats” ERG) on flexibility, open dialogue, and belonging.
  • The silent struggles of workers with ADHD — BBC Worklife — Surfaces the often-invisible daily friction ADHD workers absorb at work.
  • Mentra: Neurodiversity Employment Network — A hiring platform that matches neurodivergent strengths to roles rather than screening them out.
  • ADHD workplace accommodations need to include scrapping 9–5 (TikTok) — A short argument that rigid Mon–Fri 9–5 structures are themselves the accessibility barrier.

10. Neurodiversity policy, research & organisational toolkits

  • Neurodiversity at work — CIPD — Foundational UK HR guide framing autism, ADHD, dyslexia et al. as natural neurocognitive variation.
  • Neurodiversity in the workplace | Deloitte Insights — The business-case perspective, including the oft-cited productivity gains of neurodiverse teams.
  • Neurodiversity in the Workplace — E-Booklet & Training Guide (ADHD Foundation) — Reframes ADHD as “variable attention” and gives employers a training-oriented starting point.
  • IMI Neurodiversity Toolkit — A sector toolkit cataloguing neurotypes and the language/practice around them.
  • Neurodiversity A4 guide (UCU) — A concise union guidance sheet for supporting neurodivergent staff.
  • Inclusive language: words to use and avoid (GOV.UK) — Government guidance on respectful disability and neurodiversity language.
  • Neurodiversity policy template (VinciWorks) — A downloadable template for drafting an organisational neurodiversity policy.
  • Neurodiversity & Inclusion Policy (PP Network) — A model policy with concise descriptions of each neurotype’s indicative traits.
  • Neurodiversity & Inclusion Policy (360Giving) — A real organisation’s published policy, useful as a worked example.
  • Neurodiversity Policy (Croner-i) — HR-compliance framing within wider equality/wellbeing obligations.
  • Neurodiversity Policy (Broxtowe BC) — A public-sector policy example.
  • Beyond the business case: Universally designing the workplace for neurodiversity (Cambridge) — Argues for universal design over individual accommodation, and against reducing inclusion to a “business case.”
  • Organizational benefits of neurodiversity: autism & the bystander effect (Wiley) — Empirical finding that autistic employees may be less susceptible to the bystander effect.
  • Neurodiverse Workforce: The Invisible Diversity (ProQuest) — Dissertation on supporting inclusion of neurodivergent staff as an under-recognised diversity dimension.
  • The many faces of disability in evidence for policy and practice (Bristol UP) — On embracing complexity rather than flattening disability in policymaking.
  • Community views of neurodiversity, models of disability & autism intervention (Sage) — Mixed-methods study mapping shared goals and key tensions between the medical and neurodiversity models.
  • Axbey (Durham thesis) — Academic research on neurodivergence (doctoral thesis depth).
  • The Labour Party Autism and Neurodiversity Manifesto (Neurodivergent Labour) — The political-advocacy angle: a party-aligned policy platform for autistic/ND rights.
  • Manifesto for Autistic and Neurodivergent People’s Rights (Labour Policy Forum) — The formal policy-commission version of that rights manifesto.

11. Children, ethics & family

  • Top ten acts of cruelty that would cause outrage if they happened to non-autistic children (Autistic Not Weird) — A pointed ethics piece exposing the double standard in how autistic children are treated.
  • Resources — PAAutism.org (ASERT Autism Resource Guide) — A broad signposting hub of practical autism resources for individuals and families.

12. Books & long-form reads

  • UNMASKED: The Ultimate Guide to ADHD, Autism and Neurodivergence — Ellie Middleton — Ellie Middleton’s bite-sized, lived-experience guide written for “whizzy brains”; an incredible starting point for making sense of a late ADHD/autism diagnosis.
  • ADHD 2.0: Essential Strategies for Thriving with Distraction — Hallowell & Ratey — Two ADHD-specialist (and ADHD) clinicians distil the latest science into practical strategies for harnessing the ADHD brain.
  • Your Brain’s Not Broken — Tamara Rosier — A compassionate, emotions-first framework for understanding the ADHD experience and getting unstuck.
  • Living with PTSD on the Autism Spectrum — Combines lived experience and clinical insight on the under-researched overlap of autism and trauma.
  • The Autist’s Guide to the Galaxy — Clara Törnvall flips the script, explaining “normal”/neurotypical behaviour from an autistic vantage point.
  • Dirty Laundry — Richard Pink & Roxanne Emery (ADHD_Love) — A neurotypical+ADHD couple’s chapter-by-chapter, shame-reducing guide to living and loving with ADHD.
